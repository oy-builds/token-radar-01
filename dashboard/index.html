    What the radar reads

    The board is built from one sealed snapshot of every protocol DefiLlama tracks at or above $5M of TVL, fetched once and never updated. A protocol is pre-token when the record carries no token for it: no market cap, no CoinGecko id, no symbol. That is all the snapshot says — a protocol records no token; the radar never claims it has none.

    Who gets ranked

    Every protocol in the snapshot that records no token — all 342 of them, priced by the same fitted model. The snapshot's watchlist definition still does the classifying: 198 rows are independent launch candidates, and the other 144 are flagged on the board for what they are — a wrapped, staked, bridged or versioned product of another protocol, or an exchange's own page. The snapshot's own field guide draws the same line, naming WBTC and Binance staked ETH as products of another protocol rather than launches of their own. Flagged rows keep their price and their rank, carry a PRODUCT badge, and can be filtered out with the Kind filter; nothing the snapshot records is left invisible, and nothing is passed off as a launch it is not. Every one of the 40 watchlist protocols is on the board.

    The value model

    No constant in the model was picked by hand. The snapshot's own tokenized protocols — the peers whose tokens already trade — price the ones that have not launched:

    implied value = TVL × category multiple × size × momentum × seasoning

    • Category multiple. For each category, the median market-cap/TVL of its tokenized peers, with ratios winsorized to the cross-section's 5th–95th percentiles and the median shrunk toward the global median (prior strength 8), so one loud peer cannot price a category. The shrunk 25th–75th percentiles give each row its range.
    • Size. Among the peers, market cap grows sublinearly with TVL. Half of that fitted elasticity is applied around the peers' median TVL, so size tempers the multiple instead of compounding it.
    • Momentum. A joint fit of log market cap on log TVL, weekly growth and log age finds that market caps lag a one-week TVL surge — the denominator moves before any re-rating. Half of that fitted slope, clamped to ×0.90–×1.11, corrects for it: one week of movement is a correction, never a re-rating.
    • Seasoning. The same fit's age slope, half strength, clamped to ×0.85–×1.18. On this snapshot it is small and slightly favours the young; the radar reports the fit rather than assuming older is worth more.

    Open any row on the board and the detail view shows that protocol's own factors and the multiplication, so every rank on the board can be checked by hand.

    The farm plans

    Each plan is generated from the protocol's own record by code that ships in this page (dashboard/app.js) as well as in the program: its category picks the playbook, its chains pick where the steps happen and what an action costs there, its TVL picks the position size, and its age picks how many weeks the plan runs — six to ten weeks, stated in weeks and in days. A launch candidate's plan runs six steps and its detail view opens with the totals: position size, total gas, hold length. A flagged product's plan is five steps of a different kind: it says what the row is, where the exposure actually sits, and what holding the product anyway would cost — because there is no launch of that row to farm. Every step states its cost and its time. A plan is a starting routine, not financial advice.
